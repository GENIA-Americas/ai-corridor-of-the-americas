# The Manifesto

AI built for one market doesn't transfer cleanly to another. It has to be adapted — to a different economy, a different regulatory environment, a different set of languages and assumptions. Right now, most of that adaptation work isn't happening. AI arrives in this hemisphere finished, and the region is left to work around whatever it doesn't fit.

We think that's backwards.

## The comparison worth making, carefully

In 1971, a table-tennis exchange between American and Chinese athletes did something formal diplomacy hadn't managed in over twenty years: it opened a channel. Not because ping-pong mattered. Because it gave two sides a low-stakes, high-visibility reason to be in the same room, and everything else followed from that.

We don't think AI needs a gimmick to get people in the same room. The stakes are the opposite of low. But the underlying idea — that durable cooperation starts with a concrete, shared project rather than a treaty — is the one we've borrowed. The AI Corridor of the Americas is the shared project. Governments, universities, founders, and capital don't have to agree on everything to build shared AI infrastructure together. They just have to agree that a hemisphere this diverse is stronger with a coordination layer than without one.

## What we're building, and what we're not

We are building:
- A regional data foundation, so models are trained on data that reflects the region using them
- A talent pipeline that starts in classrooms, not in venture term sheets
- A policy track record, including formal engagement with the U.S. Congress on a regional AI strategy
- A cross-border technology platform (Glápagos) that makes multi-jurisdiction AI deployment tractable instead of theoretical

We are not building:
- A claim that any single country or company leads this alone
- A "leapfrog" narrative — this is closer to plumbing than to a moonshot, and we'd rather be honest about that than inflate it for a stage
- A closed, walled-off alternative to global AI — the goal is for this hemisphere's data and talent to be *included* in the global picture, not separated from it

## Who this is for

If you build AI, teach it, fund it, or govern it anywhere in the Western Hemisphere, this is your invitation to treat regional AI infrastructure as shared work rather than something you wait for someone else to finish. See the main [README](../README.md) for where to start.
